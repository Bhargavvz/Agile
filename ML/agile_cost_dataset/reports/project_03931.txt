1. Project Overview

This project addresses a critical challenge in the Healthcare domain. Post-discharge follow-up processes are inconsistent, causing preventable hospital readmissions. To respond to this challenge, the project team has set a clear business goal: Deploy a unified electronic health record system that improves care continuity across partnered facilities.

The primary target users for this system are insurance coordinators and physicians. The solution is designed to streamline their workflows and deliver measurable value within 3 months of development.

2. Agile Framework

The project follows the Scrum framework, which organises work into fixed-length iterations known as sprints. Each sprint lasts 2 weeks, giving the team a steady cadence for planning, development, review, and retrospective activities.

Scrum roles have been clearly defined. A Product Owner is responsible for maintaining and prioritising the product backlog in collaboration with stakeholders. A Scrum Master facilitates ceremonies, removes impediments, and coaches the team on Agile practices. The Development Team of 5 members is cross-functional, comprising front-end and back-end developers, QA engineers, and — where needed — DevOps specialists.

The incremental delivery approach ensures that a potentially shippable product increment is available at the end of every sprint. This allows stakeholders to provide feedback early and often, reducing the cost of late-stage requirement changes.

3. Product Backlog & User Stories

The product backlog contains 13 user stories that have been identified, estimated, and prioritised during initial backlog grooming sessions. Story-point estimates follow the Fibonacci-like scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) and reflect the relative complexity, effort, and uncertainty of each item. The average story-point value across the backlog is approximately 2.5.

  - As a physician, I want to access a patient's full medical history so that I can make informed clinical decisions. [8 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to link wearable device data to my profile so that my physician can monitor vitals remotely. [5 story points]
  - As a physician, I want to view population-health dashboards so that I can identify community health trends. [5 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to produce department-wise performance reports so that hospital leadership can optimise operations. [2 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to schedule preventive-care outreach campaigns so that at-risk populations are proactively contacted. [3 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to rate and review healthcare providers so that other patients can make informed choices. [1 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to request a prescription refill so that I can continue treatment without an extra visit. [13 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to manage multi-facility resource sharing so that specialist services are available across locations. [1 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to enforce data-access audit trails so that compliance with HIPAA regulations is ensured. [13 story points]
  - As a physician, I want to write and share e-prescriptions so that pharmacies can dispense medications promptly. [2 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to track medical equipment inventory so that supplies are restocked before they run out. [8 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to view my lab test results so that I stay informed about my health status. [1 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to configure automatic appointment reminders so that no-show rates are reduced. [2 story points]

4. Sprint Planning & Velocity

The project spans 3 months and is divided into 6 sprints of 2 weeks each. Based on the team's estimated capacity and historical data from similar projects, the anticipated velocity is approximately 10.0 story points per sprint.

During sprint planning, the Product Owner presents the highest-priority items from the backlog, and the Development Team selects stories whose combined estimates fit within the sprint velocity. Any incomplete stories are carried over to the next sprint after a brief retrospective discussion to identify the root cause.

Sprint reviews provide stakeholders with a live demonstration of new features. Sprint retrospectives capture lessons learned and actionable improvements, fostering continuous process enhancement.

5. System Architecture & Modules

The architecture favours simplicity and rapid delivery. A monolithic application structure keeps the codebase manageable for a small team while still supporting the planned feature set.

The following core modules have been identified:

  - User Authentication: Simple email/password login with session management
  - Dashboard: Basic summary view with key metrics
  - CRUD Module: Standard create, read, update, delete operations for core entities
  - Reporting: Pre-defined report templates with CSV export

6. Technology Stack

Frontend : Bootstrap + jQuery
Backend  : Node.js + Express
Database : SQLite
Cloud    : Railway

The stack is intentionally kept straightforward — no AI, ML, or real-time components are required. This positions the technical difficulty at 'Basic', keeping the cost-per-hour and effort multipliers at their baseline values.

7. Risk & Requirement Volatility

The overall risk level for this project is assessed as 'Low', and requirement volatility is rated 'Low'. Requirements have been well-defined upfront, and only minor refinements are anticipated during later sprints. 

Key risks identified:
  - Minor scope adjustments expected during later sprints.
  - Potential minor delays if key team members take planned leave.

Mitigation strategies adopted by the team:
  - Maintain a spike backlog for research tasks to de-risk unknowns early.
  - Automate regression tests to catch breakages from dependency updates.
  - Schedule knowledge-transfer sessions to reduce bus-factor risk.
  - Conduct bi-weekly stakeholder review sessions to catch requirement drift.
