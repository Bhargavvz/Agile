1. Project Overview

This project addresses a critical challenge in the IoT domain. Smart-city sensor networks generate massive telemetry but cities lack platforms to derive actionable insights. To respond to this challenge, the project team has set a clear business goal: Deploy a centralised equipment-monitoring platform that reduces unplanned downtime by 45%.

The primary target users for this system are city planners and facility managers. The solution is designed to streamline their workflows and deliver measurable value within 10 months of development.

2. Agile Framework

The project follows the Scrum framework, which organises work into fixed-length iterations known as sprints. Each sprint lasts 2 weeks, giving the team a steady cadence for planning, development, review, and retrospective activities.

Scrum roles have been clearly defined. A Product Owner is responsible for maintaining and prioritising the product backlog in collaboration with stakeholders. A Scrum Master facilitates ceremonies, removes impediments, and coaches the team on Agile practices. The Development Team of 10 members is cross-functional, comprising front-end and back-end developers, QA engineers, and — where needed — DevOps specialists.

The incremental delivery approach ensures that a potentially shippable product increment is available at the end of every sprint. This allows stakeholders to provide feedback early and often, reducing the cost of late-stage requirement changes.

4. Sprint Planning & Velocity

The project spans 10 months and is divided into 20 sprints of 2 weeks each. Based on the team's estimated capacity and historical data from similar projects, the anticipated velocity is approximately 53.6 story points per sprint.

During sprint planning, the Product Owner presents the highest-priority items from the backlog, and the Development Team selects stories whose combined estimates fit within the sprint velocity. Any incomplete stories are carried over to the next sprint after a brief retrospective discussion to identify the root cause.

Sprint reviews provide stakeholders with a live demonstration of new features. Sprint retrospectives capture lessons learned and actionable improvements, fostering continuous process enhancement.

5. System Architecture & Modules

A microservices architecture has been selected to handle the system's scale and complexity. Individual services can be developed, tested, and deployed independently, enabling the larger team to work in parallel without merge conflicts or coupled release cycles.

The following core modules have been identified:

  - Microservice Orchestrator: Service mesh with load balancing and circuit breakers
  - Real-time Data Pipeline: Event-driven ingestion using message queues
  - ML Inference Service: Model serving with A/B testing and canary deployments
  - Identity & Access Management: SSO, MFA, and fine-grained permission policies
  - Workflow Engine: Configurable, state-machine-based business-process automation
  - Audit & Compliance Logger: Immutable audit trail for regulatory compliance
  - Data Lake Integration: ETL pipelines connecting operational and analytical stores
  - Edge Computing Gateway: Lightweight processing at the network edge for IoT devices

6. Technology Stack

Frontend : React.js + TypeScript
Backend  : FastAPI + Celery
Database : PostgreSQL + Redis
Cloud    : Azure AKS + Functions

Additional capabilities integrated into the stack include real-time WebSocket streaming, ML-powered recommendation engine, end-to-end encryption and AI-based anomaly detection. These features elevate the technical difficulty to 'Advanced' and are reflected in the effort estimation multiplier.

7. Risk & Requirement Volatility

The overall risk level for this project is assessed as 'High', and requirement volatility is rated 'High'. Stakeholder priorities are expected to shift during the course of the project, which may lead to backlog re-prioritisation and scope adjustments across sprints. 

Key risks identified:
  - Regulatory changes may necessitate significant architecture redesign.
  - High requirement volatility from multiple stakeholder groups.
  - Integration with legacy systems carries substantial technical debt.
  - Data-privacy regulations may impose additional encryption and audit requirements mid-project.

Mitigation strategies adopted by the team:
  - Keep a 15% time buffer in each sprint for unplanned work.
  - Implement feature flags to isolate risky changes behind toggles.
  - Schedule knowledge-transfer sessions to reduce bus-factor risk.
  - Use contract testing for third-party API integrations.
