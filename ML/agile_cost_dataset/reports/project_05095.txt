1. Project Overview

This project addresses a critical challenge in the Healthcare domain. Rural communities lack timely access to specialist consultations, resulting in delayed diagnoses and treatment. To respond to this challenge, the project team has set a clear business goal: Automate appointment scheduling and follow-up reminders to reduce no-show rates by 30%.

The primary target users for this system are physicians, insurance coordinators and patients. The solution is designed to streamline their workflows and deliver measurable value within 4 months of development.

2. Agile Framework

The project follows the Scrum framework, which organises work into fixed-length iterations known as sprints. Each sprint lasts 2 weeks, giving the team a steady cadence for planning, development, review, and retrospective activities.

Scrum roles have been clearly defined. A Product Owner is responsible for maintaining and prioritising the product backlog in collaboration with stakeholders. A Scrum Master facilitates ceremonies, removes impediments, and coaches the team on Agile practices. The Development Team of 4 members is cross-functional, comprising front-end and back-end developers, QA engineers, and — where needed — DevOps specialists.

The incremental delivery approach ensures that a potentially shippable product increment is available at the end of every sprint. This allows stakeholders to provide feedback early and often, reducing the cost of late-stage requirement changes.

3. Product Backlog & User Stories

The product backlog contains 13 user stories that have been identified, estimated, and prioritised during initial backlog grooming sessions. Story-point estimates follow the Fibonacci-like scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) and reflect the relative complexity, effort, and uncertainty of each item. The average story-point value across the backlog is approximately 3.3.

  - As a physician, I want to collaborate with another physician on a case so that complex cases get multi-specialist input. [2 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to rate and review healthcare providers so that other patients can make informed choices. [5 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to manage doctor schedules so that appointment slots are allocated efficiently. [8 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to link wearable device data to my profile so that my physician can monitor vitals remotely. [5 story points]
  - As a physician, I want to order lab tests electronically so that results are linked to the patient's record automatically. [1 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to configure automatic appointment reminders so that no-show rates are reduced. [8 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to update my insurance information so that billing is processed without delays. [8 story points]
  - As a physician, I want to view diagnostic imaging inline so that I can discuss findings with patients in real time. [8 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to generate insurance claim reports so that billing processes are completed on time. [8 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to monitor bed occupancy in real time so that resources are allocated where needed most. [3 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to track medical equipment inventory so that supplies are restocked before they run out. [5 story points]
  - As a physician, I want to access drug interaction warnings so that I prescribe medications safely. [13 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to enforce data-access audit trails so that compliance with HIPAA regulations is ensured. [8 story points]

4. Sprint Planning & Velocity

The project spans 4 months and is divided into 8 sprints of 2 weeks each. Based on the team's estimated capacity and historical data from similar projects, the anticipated velocity is approximately 10.6 story points per sprint.

During sprint planning, the Product Owner presents the highest-priority items from the backlog, and the Development Team selects stories whose combined estimates fit within the sprint velocity. Any incomplete stories are carried over to the next sprint after a brief retrospective discussion to identify the root cause.

Sprint reviews provide stakeholders with a live demonstration of new features. Sprint retrospectives capture lessons learned and actionable improvements, fostering continuous process enhancement.

5. System Architecture & Modules

The architecture favours simplicity and rapid delivery. A monolithic application structure keeps the codebase manageable for a small team while still supporting the planned feature set.

The following core modules have been identified:

  - User Authentication: Simple email/password login with session management
  - Dashboard: Basic summary view with key metrics
  - CRUD Module: Standard create, read, update, delete operations for core entities
  - Reporting: Pre-defined report templates with CSV export

6. Technology Stack

Frontend : HTML/CSS/JS
Backend  : Node.js + Express
Database : MySQL
Cloud    : Heroku

The stack is intentionally kept straightforward — no AI, ML, or real-time components are required. This positions the technical difficulty at 'Basic', keeping the cost-per-hour and effort multipliers at their baseline values.

7. Risk & Requirement Volatility

The overall risk level for this project is assessed as 'Low', and requirement volatility is rated 'Low'. Requirements have been well-defined upfront, and only minor refinements are anticipated during later sprints. 

Key risks identified:
  - Minor scope adjustments expected during later sprints.
  - Potential minor delays if key team members take planned leave.

Mitigation strategies adopted by the team:
  - Establish a formal change-request process to manage scope creep.
  - Schedule knowledge-transfer sessions to reduce bus-factor risk.
