1. Project Overview

This project addresses a critical challenge in the E-commerce domain. Existing e-commerce platforms fail to provide personalised shopping experiences, leading to high cart-abandonment rates. To respond to this challenge, the project team has set a clear business goal: Enable omnichannel sales management so merchants can synchronise inventory across all channels in real time.

The primary target users for this system are retail merchants, online shoppers and warehouse managers. The solution is designed to streamline their workflows and deliver measurable value within 3 months of development.

2. Agile Framework

The project follows the Scrum framework, which organises work into fixed-length iterations known as sprints. Each sprint lasts 2 weeks, giving the team a steady cadence for planning, development, review, and retrospective activities.

Scrum roles have been clearly defined. A Product Owner is responsible for maintaining and prioritising the product backlog in collaboration with stakeholders. A Scrum Master facilitates ceremonies, removes impediments, and coaches the team on Agile practices. The Development Team of 4 members is cross-functional, comprising front-end and back-end developers, QA engineers, and — where needed — DevOps specialists.

The incremental delivery approach ensures that a potentially shippable product increment is available at the end of every sprint. This allows stakeholders to provide feedback early and often, reducing the cost of late-stage requirement changes.

3. Product Backlog & User Stories

The product backlog contains 10 user stories that have been identified, estimated, and prioritised during initial backlog grooming sessions. Story-point estimates follow the Fibonacci-like scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) and reflect the relative complexity, effort, and uncertainty of each item. The average story-point value across the backlog is approximately 3.9.

  - As a shopper, I want to share a product via social media so that friends can see items I recommend. [5 story points]
  - As a merchant, I want to offer flash sales with countdown timers so that I can create urgency and boost conversions. [1 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to send platform-wide announcements so that all users are informed of important updates. [5 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to audit user activity logs so that I can detect and prevent fraudulent behaviour. [3 story points]
  - As a shopper, I want to browse products by category so that I can quickly find what I need. [1 story points]
  - As a merchant, I want to export product data to CSV so that I can use the data in external tools. [8 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to configure tax and compliance rules so that the platform meets regional regulations. [1 story points]
  - As a shopper, I want to subscribe to a product category newsletter so that I stay informed about new arrivals. [5 story points]
  - As a merchant, I want to set up automated promotional campaigns so that I can boost sales without manual effort. [8 story points]
  - As a shopper, I want to filter search results by price range so that I find products within my budget quickly. [3 story points]

4. Sprint Planning & Velocity

The project spans 3 months and is divided into 6 sprints of 2 weeks each. Based on the team's estimated capacity and historical data from similar projects, the anticipated velocity is approximately 12.5 story points per sprint.

During sprint planning, the Product Owner presents the highest-priority items from the backlog, and the Development Team selects stories whose combined estimates fit within the sprint velocity. Any incomplete stories are carried over to the next sprint after a brief retrospective discussion to identify the root cause.

Sprint reviews provide stakeholders with a live demonstration of new features. Sprint retrospectives capture lessons learned and actionable improvements, fostering continuous process enhancement.

5. System Architecture & Modules

The architecture favours simplicity and rapid delivery. A monolithic application structure keeps the codebase manageable for a small team while still supporting the planned feature set.

The following core modules have been identified:

  - User Authentication: Simple email/password login with session management
  - Dashboard: Basic summary view with key metrics
  - CRUD Module: Standard create, read, update, delete operations for core entities
  - Reporting: Pre-defined report templates with CSV export

6. Technology Stack

Frontend : Bootstrap + jQuery
Backend  : Node.js + Express
Database : PostgreSQL
Cloud    : Render

The stack is intentionally kept straightforward — no AI, ML, or real-time components are required. This positions the technical difficulty at 'Basic', keeping the cost-per-hour and effort multipliers at their baseline values.

7. Risk & Requirement Volatility

The overall risk level for this project is assessed as 'Low', and requirement volatility is rated 'Low'. Requirements have been well-defined upfront, and only minor refinements are anticipated during later sprints. 

Key risks identified:
  - Minor scope adjustments expected during later sprints.
  - Potential minor delays if key team members take planned leave.

Mitigation strategies adopted by the team:
  - Conduct bi-weekly stakeholder review sessions to catch requirement drift.
  - Establish a formal change-request process to manage scope creep.
  - Automate regression tests to catch breakages from dependency updates.
