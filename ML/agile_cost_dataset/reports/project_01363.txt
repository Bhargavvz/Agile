1. Project Overview

This project addresses a critical challenge in the IoT domain. Industrial manufacturers lack centralised monitoring of equipment health, leading to unplanned downtime. To respond to this challenge, the project team has set a clear business goal: Deploy a centralised equipment-monitoring platform that reduces unplanned downtime by 45%.

The primary target users for this system are field technicians and facility managers. The solution is designed to streamline their workflows and deliver measurable value within 2 months of development.

2. Agile Framework

The project follows the Scrum framework, which organises work into fixed-length iterations known as sprints. Each sprint lasts 2 weeks, giving the team a steady cadence for planning, development, review, and retrospective activities.

Scrum roles have been clearly defined. A Product Owner is responsible for maintaining and prioritising the product backlog in collaboration with stakeholders. A Scrum Master facilitates ceremonies, removes impediments, and coaches the team on Agile practices. The Development Team of 5 members is cross-functional, comprising front-end and back-end developers, QA engineers, and — where needed — DevOps specialists.

The incremental delivery approach ensures that a potentially shippable product increment is available at the end of every sprint. This allows stakeholders to provide feedback early and often, reducing the cost of late-stage requirement changes.

3. Product Backlog & User Stories

The product backlog contains 13 user stories that have been identified, estimated, and prioritised during initial backlog grooming sessions. Story-point estimates follow the Fibonacci-like scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) and reflect the relative complexity, effort, and uncertainty of each item. The average story-point value across the backlog is approximately 2.6.

  - As a technician, I want to view real-time sensor readings so that I can assess equipment status from anywhere. [5 story points]
  - As a technician, I want to run remote diagnostics so that root-cause analysis does not require a site visit. [1 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to enforce secure device authentication so that only authorised devices connect to the platform. [8 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to configure data retention policies so that storage costs and compliance are balanced. [13 story points]
  - As a manager, I want to schedule predictive maintenance tasks so that equipment reliability improves over time. [3 story points]
  - As a manager, I want to set energy-saving automation rules so that operational costs are reduced automatically. [13 story points]
  - As a technician, I want to receive step-by-step repair guides so that complex repairs are completed correctly. [1 story points]
  - As a manager, I want to configure automated shutdown procedures so that safety protocols are enforced automatically. [13 story points]
  - As a manager, I want to view environmental condition maps so that facility conditions are monitored holistically. [1 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to manage OTA update rollout schedules so that firmware deployments are controlled and safe. [2 story points]
  - As a technician, I want to calibrate sensors remotely so that measurement accuracy is maintained without site visits. [8 story points]
  - As a technician, I want to view wiring and schematic diagrams so that maintenance is guided by accurate documentation. [13 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to manage device grouping and tagging so that assets are organised logically. [13 story points]

4. Sprint Planning & Velocity

The project spans 2 months and is divided into 4 sprints of 2 weeks each. Based on the team's estimated capacity and historical data from similar projects, the anticipated velocity is approximately 10.4 story points per sprint.

During sprint planning, the Product Owner presents the highest-priority items from the backlog, and the Development Team selects stories whose combined estimates fit within the sprint velocity. Any incomplete stories are carried over to the next sprint after a brief retrospective discussion to identify the root cause.

Sprint reviews provide stakeholders with a live demonstration of new features. Sprint retrospectives capture lessons learned and actionable improvements, fostering continuous process enhancement.

5. System Architecture & Modules

The architecture favours simplicity and rapid delivery. A monolithic application structure keeps the codebase manageable for a small team while still supporting the planned feature set.

The following core modules have been identified:

  - User Authentication: Simple email/password login with session management
  - Dashboard: Basic summary view with key metrics
  - CRUD Module: Standard create, read, update, delete operations for core entities
  - Reporting: Pre-defined report templates with CSV export

6. Technology Stack

Frontend : Vue.js
Backend  : Node.js + Express
Database : SQLite
Cloud    : Render

The stack is intentionally kept straightforward — no AI, ML, or real-time components are required. This positions the technical difficulty at 'Basic', keeping the cost-per-hour and effort multipliers at their baseline values.

7. Risk & Requirement Volatility

The overall risk level for this project is assessed as 'Low', and requirement volatility is rated 'Medium'. Stakeholder priorities are expected to shift during the course of the project, which may lead to backlog re-prioritisation and scope adjustments across sprints. 

Key risks identified:
  - Minor scope adjustments expected during later sprints.
  - Potential minor delays if key team members take planned leave.

Mitigation strategies adopted by the team:
  - Maintain a spike backlog for research tasks to de-risk unknowns early.
  - Conduct bi-weekly stakeholder review sessions to catch requirement drift.
