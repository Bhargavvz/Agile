1. Project Overview

This project addresses a critical challenge in the Healthcare domain. Patient medical records are fragmented across facilities, making continuity of care extremely challenging for practitioners. To respond to this challenge, the project team has set a clear business goal: Automate appointment scheduling and follow-up reminders to reduce no-show rates by 30%.

The primary target users for this system are physicians, patients and hospital administrators. The solution is designed to streamline their workflows and deliver measurable value within 3 months of development.

2. Agile Framework

The project follows the Scrum framework, which organises work into fixed-length iterations known as sprints. Each sprint lasts 2 weeks, giving the team a steady cadence for planning, development, review, and retrospective activities.

Scrum roles have been clearly defined. A Product Owner is responsible for maintaining and prioritising the product backlog in collaboration with stakeholders. A Scrum Master facilitates ceremonies, removes impediments, and coaches the team on Agile practices. The Development Team of 3 members is cross-functional, comprising front-end and back-end developers, QA engineers, and — where needed — DevOps specialists.

The incremental delivery approach ensures that a potentially shippable product increment is available at the end of every sprint. This allows stakeholders to provide feedback early and often, reducing the cost of late-stage requirement changes.

3. Product Backlog & User Stories

The product backlog contains 12 user stories that have been identified, estimated, and prioritised during initial backlog grooming sessions. Story-point estimates follow the Fibonacci-like scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) and reflect the relative complexity, effort, and uncertainty of each item. The average story-point value across the backlog is approximately 3.0.

  - As a physician, I want to set up clinical alerts for critical values so that I am notified immediately of emergencies. [13 story points]
  - As a physician, I want to write and share e-prescriptions so that pharmacies can dispense medications promptly. [8 story points]
  - As a physician, I want to collaborate with another physician on a case so that complex cases get multi-specialist input. [13 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to monitor bed occupancy in real time so that resources are allocated where needed most. [1 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to receive medication reminders so that I never miss a dose. [1 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to consult a specialist via video call so that I get expert advice without travelling. [5 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to view my lab test results so that I stay informed about my health status. [1 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to update my insurance information so that billing is processed without delays. [5 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to schedule preventive-care outreach campaigns so that at-risk populations are proactively contacted. [5 story points]
  - As a physician, I want to access drug interaction warnings so that I prescribe medications safely. [8 story points]
  - As a physician, I want to document clinical notes with voice input so that charting time is reduced significantly. [13 story points]
  - As a patient, I want to set health goals and track progress so that I am more engaged in my wellness journey. [8 story points]

4. Sprint Planning & Velocity

The project spans 3 months and is divided into 6 sprints of 2 weeks each. Based on the team's estimated capacity and historical data from similar projects, the anticipated velocity is approximately 7.2 story points per sprint.

During sprint planning, the Product Owner presents the highest-priority items from the backlog, and the Development Team selects stories whose combined estimates fit within the sprint velocity. Any incomplete stories are carried over to the next sprint after a brief retrospective discussion to identify the root cause.

Sprint reviews provide stakeholders with a live demonstration of new features. Sprint retrospectives capture lessons learned and actionable improvements, fostering continuous process enhancement.

5. System Architecture & Modules

The architecture favours simplicity and rapid delivery. A monolithic application structure keeps the codebase manageable for a small team while still supporting the planned feature set.

The following core modules have been identified:

  - User Authentication: Simple email/password login with session management
  - Dashboard: Basic summary view with key metrics
  - CRUD Module: Standard create, read, update, delete operations for core entities
  - Reporting: Pre-defined report templates with CSV export

6. Technology Stack

Frontend : HTML/CSS/JS
Backend  : Node.js + Express
Database : MySQL
Cloud    : Railway

The stack is intentionally kept straightforward — no AI, ML, or real-time components are required. This positions the technical difficulty at 'Basic', keeping the cost-per-hour and effort multipliers at their baseline values.

7. Risk & Requirement Volatility

The overall risk level for this project is assessed as 'Low', and requirement volatility is rated 'Medium'. Stakeholder priorities are expected to shift during the course of the project, which may lead to backlog re-prioritisation and scope adjustments across sprints. 

Key risks identified:
  - Minor scope adjustments expected during later sprints.
  - Potential minor delays if key team members take planned leave.

Mitigation strategies adopted by the team:
  - Implement feature flags to isolate risky changes behind toggles.
  - Establish a formal change-request process to manage scope creep.
  - Automate regression tests to catch breakages from dependency updates.
  - Schedule knowledge-transfer sessions to reduce bus-factor risk.
