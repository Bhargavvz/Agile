1. Project Overview

This project addresses a critical challenge in the E-commerce domain. Traditional brick-and-mortar retailers are losing market share to online competitors due to lack of digital presence. To respond to this challenge, the project team has set a clear business goal: Launch a feature-rich online marketplace that drives a 40% increase in customer reach within 12 months.

The primary target users for this system are warehouse managers and online shoppers. The solution is designed to streamline their workflows and deliver measurable value within 4 months of development.

2. Agile Framework

The project follows the Scrum framework, which organises work into fixed-length iterations known as sprints. Each sprint lasts 2 weeks, giving the team a steady cadence for planning, development, review, and retrospective activities.

Scrum roles have been clearly defined. A Product Owner is responsible for maintaining and prioritising the product backlog in collaboration with stakeholders. A Scrum Master facilitates ceremonies, removes impediments, and coaches the team on Agile practices. The Development Team of 3 members is cross-functional, comprising front-end and back-end developers, QA engineers, and — where needed — DevOps specialists.

The incremental delivery approach ensures that a potentially shippable product increment is available at the end of every sprint. This allows stakeholders to provide feedback early and often, reducing the cost of late-stage requirement changes.

3. Product Backlog & User Stories

The product backlog contains 15 user stories that have been identified, estimated, and prioritised during initial backlog grooming sessions. Story-point estimates follow the Fibonacci-like scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) and reflect the relative complexity, effort, and uncertainty of each item. The average story-point value across the backlog is approximately 3.2.

  - As a shopper, I want to compare products side by side so that I can choose the best option for my budget. [5 story points]
  - As a shopper, I want to use multiple payment methods so that I have flexibility at checkout. [3 story points]
  - As a shopper, I want to share a product via social media so that friends can see items I recommend. [5 story points]
  - As a shopper, I want to receive personalised product recommendations so that I discover items I am likely to buy. [8 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to generate financial settlement reports so that I can reconcile payments with vendors. [13 story points]
  - As a shopper, I want to add items to a wish list so that I can save products for later purchase. [2 story points]
  - As a merchant, I want to offer flash sales with countdown timers so that I can create urgency and boost conversions. [2 story points]
  - As a merchant, I want to set up automated promotional campaigns so that I can boost sales without manual effort. [3 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to audit user activity logs so that I can detect and prevent fraudulent behaviour. [3 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to send platform-wide announcements so that all users are informed of important updates. [5 story points]
  - As a shopper, I want to leave a product review so that other shoppers can benefit from my experience. [8 story points]
  - As a shopper, I want to chat with customer support so that I can get help without leaving the site. [3 story points]
  - As a merchant, I want to export product data to CSV so that I can use the data in external tools. [13 story points]
  - As a merchant, I want to offer bundle deals so that I can increase average order value. [1 story points]
  - As a admin, I want to configure role-based access policies so that staff see only the data they need. [1 story points]

4. Sprint Planning & Velocity

The project spans 4 months and is divided into 8 sprints of 2 weeks each. Based on the team's estimated capacity and historical data from similar projects, the anticipated velocity is approximately 7.7 story points per sprint.

During sprint planning, the Product Owner presents the highest-priority items from the backlog, and the Development Team selects stories whose combined estimates fit within the sprint velocity. Any incomplete stories are carried over to the next sprint after a brief retrospective discussion to identify the root cause.

Sprint reviews provide stakeholders with a live demonstration of new features. Sprint retrospectives capture lessons learned and actionable improvements, fostering continuous process enhancement.

5. System Architecture & Modules

The architecture favours simplicity and rapid delivery. A monolithic application structure keeps the codebase manageable for a small team while still supporting the planned feature set.

The following core modules have been identified:

  - User Authentication: Simple email/password login with session management
  - Dashboard: Basic summary view with key metrics
  - CRUD Module: Standard create, read, update, delete operations for core entities
  - Reporting: Pre-defined report templates with CSV export

6. Technology Stack

Frontend : HTML/CSS/JS
Backend  : Node.js + Express
Database : SQLite
Cloud    : Heroku

The stack is intentionally kept straightforward — no AI, ML, or real-time components are required. This positions the technical difficulty at 'Basic', keeping the cost-per-hour and effort multipliers at their baseline values.

7. Risk & Requirement Volatility

The overall risk level for this project is assessed as 'Low', and requirement volatility is rated 'Medium'. Stakeholder priorities are expected to shift during the course of the project, which may lead to backlog re-prioritisation and scope adjustments across sprints. 

Key risks identified:
  - Minor scope adjustments expected during later sprints.
  - Potential minor delays if key team members take planned leave.

Mitigation strategies adopted by the team:
  - Schedule knowledge-transfer sessions to reduce bus-factor risk.
  - Conduct bi-weekly stakeholder review sessions to catch requirement drift.
  - Maintain a spike backlog for research tasks to de-risk unknowns early.
