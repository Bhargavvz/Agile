1. Project Overview

This project addresses a critical challenge in the Healthcare domain. Rural communities lack timely access to specialist consultations, resulting in delayed diagnoses and treatment. To respond to this challenge, the project team has set a clear business goal: Deploy a unified electronic health record system that improves care continuity across partnered facilities.

The primary target users for this system are patients and hospital administrators. The solution is designed to streamline their workflows and deliver measurable value within 6 months of development.

2. Agile Framework

The project follows the Scrum framework, which organises work into fixed-length iterations known as sprints. Each sprint lasts 2 weeks, giving the team a steady cadence for planning, development, review, and retrospective activities.

Scrum roles have been clearly defined. A Product Owner is responsible for maintaining and prioritising the product backlog in collaboration with stakeholders. A Scrum Master facilitates ceremonies, removes impediments, and coaches the team on Agile practices. The Development Team of 5 members is cross-functional, comprising front-end and back-end developers, QA engineers, and — where needed — DevOps specialists.

The incremental delivery approach ensures that a potentially shippable product increment is available at the end of every sprint. This allows stakeholders to provide feedback early and often, reducing the cost of late-stage requirement changes.

4. Sprint Planning & Velocity

The project spans 6 months and is divided into 12 sprints of 2 weeks each. Based on the team's estimated capacity and historical data from similar projects, the anticipated velocity is approximately 24.0 story points per sprint.

During sprint planning, the Product Owner presents the highest-priority items from the backlog, and the Development Team selects stories whose combined estimates fit within the sprint velocity. Any incomplete stories are carried over to the next sprint after a brief retrospective discussion to identify the root cause.

Sprint reviews provide stakeholders with a live demonstration of new features. Sprint retrospectives capture lessons learned and actionable improvements, fostering continuous process enhancement.

5. System Architecture & Modules

A modular architecture separates concerns across well-defined service boundaries. This balances development velocity with the flexibility needed to accommodate growing feature requirements as the product matures.

The following core modules have been identified:

  - User & Role Management: Role-based access control with multi-tenancy support
  - API Gateway: Centralised routing, rate limiting, and request validation
  - Notification Service: Email, SMS, and in-app notification delivery engine
  - Analytics Engine: Aggregated dashboards with drill-down and date-range filtering
  - Payment Integration: Secure payment processing via third-party gateway
  - Search Module: Full-text search with filters and pagination

6. Technology Stack

Frontend : Next.js
Backend  : FastAPI
Database : PostgreSQL
Cloud    : AWS EC2 + S3

Additional capabilities integrated into the stack include RESTful API gateway, OAuth 2.0 authentication and cloud-based CI/CD pipeline. These features elevate the technical difficulty to 'Intermediate' and are reflected in the effort estimation multiplier.

7. Risk & Requirement Volatility

The overall risk level for this project is assessed as 'Medium', and requirement volatility is rated 'Medium'. Stakeholder priorities are expected to shift during the course of the project, which may lead to backlog re-prioritisation and scope adjustments across sprints. 

Key risks identified:
  - Third-party API changes could require interface rework.
  - Stakeholder requirement revisions expected after UAT phase.
  - Team may face learning-curve delays with new framework adoption.

Mitigation strategies adopted by the team:
  - Automate regression tests to catch breakages from dependency updates.
  - Maintain a spike backlog for research tasks to de-risk unknowns early.
  - Keep a 15% time buffer in each sprint for unplanned work.
